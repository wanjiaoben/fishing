A Full Day Out of Chatan: 25 Fish, an Electric Reel and a Cooler That Wouldn't Close

Trip report — Private full-day charter, 13 August 2026, Chatan Port

Four guests from the US, one full day on the water. The rest of their group had a concert in Osaka the next day; for one of them, the real reason for the trip was this boat.

He is not a beginner. Back home he fishes big game with Shimano Talica reels and knows his gear. What he wanted from us was simple: a local captain, a boat, and someone to handle the Japanese side of things so he could just fish.

How the booking went

Everything happened in English over email and WhatsApp. We confirmed the date, the boat and the plan, sent a confirmation email with the meeting point and the final total, and placed a card hold for the amount — no deposit, nothing charged up front. The hold was released after the trip.

One honest note from our side: we ask guests to confirm the meeting time the evening before. This time the "We'll be there at 6 AM" reply landed at 4 in the morning. Everyone showed up on time and the day was great — but our staff and the captain had a long, quiet night refreshing their inboxes. Please reply the night before. We'll sleep better.

It's a small thing, but after a few seasons we've noticed a pattern. Guests from East Asia tend to message at every step — "leaving the hotel now", "in the taxi", "we see the boat" — and go quiet only once they're on board. Guests from North America and Europe tend to confirm once, then simply show up on time. Neither is wrong; they're just different habits. We've learned to read both, and we only ask one thing of everyone: one reply the evening before, so the captain knows the day is on.

The day

  • 06:00 Met at Chatan Port. Life jackets, a quick chat with the captain about what was biting that week, and out past the reef.
  • Morning Lines in. The captain set everyone up with rental rods and the local rig.

This is where it got interesting for our angler. Okinawa deep-water fishing uses a weight setup he had never seen before, and the boat runs electric reels — new technology for someone used to cranking Talicas by hand. He picked it up within a couple of drops and, in his words, learned a few things.

The fish

The count at the end of the day:

Tuna (maguro)20, around 2–3 kg each
Rainbow runner (tsumuburi)4, around 2–3 kg each
Amberjack (kanpachi)1, about 3 kg

The tuna came steadily from the first drops — the electric reel earned its keep there. The amberjack was the one that got the photo: brought up on the electric reel and held up with the kind of grin that needs no caption. The younger members of the group landed plenty of the tuna too — not their main reason for being in Japan, but they had a good time.

  • 17:00 Back at Chatan with 25 fish on ice in the styrofoam box we had ready — theirs to keep.
  • That afternoon we had a table booked at a local restaurant that cooks your catch — so the amberjack went from the cooler to the table the same day. We also tracked down an ATM that takes foreign cards for cash, which turned out to be the day's second-hardest catch.
  • That evening they were already on their way to Osaka.

In their words

"Thanks to you and your assistance for giving us great service. I learned a few things from you such as using the weight I have never seen before as well as using the new technology electric reel."

"We will consider going with you again if we come back and fish, as well as tell my fishing friends about you guys."

— Guest from the US, August 2026
